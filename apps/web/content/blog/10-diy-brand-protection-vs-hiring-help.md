# DIY brand protection vs. hiring help: an honest cost comparison

We sell brand protection, so take this with that in mind. We'll make the case
for doing it yourself as fairly as we can, because for a lot of brands it's the
right answer, and a customer who signs up too early cancels.

What follows is a way to work out what each option costs *you*, using your own
numbers rather than ours.

## What "brand protection" involves

Whoever does the work, it's the same four jobs:

1. **Find.** Search marketplaces, search engines, social platforms and domain
   registrations for your brand. ([The manual playbook](./01-find-counterfeits-online.md).)
2. **Judge.** Decide which results are real problems. Your authorised reseller, a
   review blog and press coverage all mention your brand, and none of them is an
   infringement. Getting this wrong is the costly mistake.
3. **Enforce.** Capture evidence, choose the right channel and notice, file it, and
   follow up.
4. **Repeat.** Do it again next week and compare, because sellers relist. ([Why
   counterfeiters relist](./06-why-counterfeiters-relist.md).)

The first and fourth jobs are mechanical. The second and third need judgement. In
practice, the cost of brand protection is mostly the cost of the judgement.

## The cost of doing it yourself

DIY has no invoice, but it isn't free. Its cost is time, and you can estimate it.

Time a full pass once, then plug in your numbers:

| Task | Your estimate |
|---|---|
| Running your search set across the platforms you care about | ___ min / week |
| Reviewing results and comparing against last week | ___ min / week |
| Capturing evidence per finding ([what to capture](./09-takedown-evidence-file.md)) | ___ min × findings |
| Filing and following up per notice | ___ min × notices |

Multiply the total by the loaded hourly cost of whoever does it. If that's a founder
or a head of marketing, it isn't cheap.

For a sense of scale: our own team budgets roughly **15 minutes of human review per
notice**, and that's with search, comparison and evidence capture already automated.
That figure is only the judgement and filing. If you're also doing everything else by
hand, assume more.

Three costs don't show up in a timesheet:

- **Inconsistency.** The weekly pass is the first thing dropped in a busy week, and
  infringement doesn't take weeks off. Gaps let listings collect sales and reviews
  before you see them.
- **Missed coverage.** Manual search stops after the first page or two of results.
  The second page of a marketplace search, or a store reached only by a
  "brand + replica" query, may never get looked at.
- **No memory.** Without a log, a relisting looks like a new problem, and you lose
  the repeat-infringer argument that gets accounts closed.

## When DIY is enough

Do it yourself if most of these describe you:

- A search pass turns up **a handful of findings**, mostly on one or two
  marketplaces.
- The findings are **clear-cut**: your name, your photos, "replica" in the title.
- Someone on the team can **reliably protect an hour or two a week** for it.
- You don't have a **phishing or impersonation** problem putting customers' money or
  accounts at risk, where speed matters more than cost.
- Your **registered trademarks** are in place, so the platform programs are
  available to you directly.

If that's you, use the playbooks on this blog, keep a spreadsheet, and come back to
the question in six months. The marketplaces' own brand-owner programs are free and
work well for a brand in this position.

## When DIY stops working

These are the signs that the time cost has passed the cost of help:

- **The weekly pass keeps getting skipped**, and you keep finding listings that have
  clearly been up for months.
- **Findings run to dozens**, and comparing against last week no longer fits in a
  spreadsheet.
- **You're seeing several categories at once**: counterfeits, plus
  [lookalike domains](./03-lookalike-domains-typosquatting.md), plus
  [fake support accounts](./07-fake-customer-support-accounts.md). Each has its own
  channels and its own learning curve.
- **The same sellers keep coming back** and you need to show a pattern to get them
  closed.
- **A domain needs escalation** to a [UDRP](./05-udrp-explained.md) or a lawyer, and
  nobody in-house has done one.

## The options for outside help

Roughly three kinds, each with real trade-offs:

**A law firm.** The right choice for litigation, customs recordals, and disputes with
real money at stake. Billing is usually hourly, which suits high-stakes work and
doesn't suit filing the same marketplace form forty times.

**An enterprise brand-protection vendor.** Broad coverage, dedicated account teams,
often built for very large brands. Many don't publish prices and work on annual
contracts. Worth asking for a quote if your volume is very large.

- **Enforcements are metered, not unlimited.** Every notice we file is reviewed by a

  legal exposure for the filer. Human review costs money per notice. An "unlimited"
  plan would either lose us money or tempt us to skip the review, and we won't do
  either.
- **We verify you own or represent the mark before filing anything.** That adds a step
  at the start. It's the same step a careful in-house team would take.

## Doing the arithmetic

Put your DIY estimate next to the tier that matches your volume:

- If your honest weekly time costs less than **$149 a month** and you're keeping up,
  stay DIY. Monitoring alone wouldn't buy you much.
- If you're keeping up with enforcement but **losing track of what's new**, Monitor
  covers the find-and-compare part and leaves filing to you.
- If you're filing **more than a handful of notices a month**, compare the time cost
  of those notices with Protect. Ten included enforcements works out at about $60 per
  notice, against $195 for a single one, so at more than three a month the plan costs less than paying
  per notice.
- If enforcement is taking **a large part of someone's week**, compare Managed with
  that person's time, not with zero.

And if you aren't sure what volume you have, which is most people before they've
looked properly, find that out first. It costs nothing.
